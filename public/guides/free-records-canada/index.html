Free genealogy records in Canada: where to look

You don’t need an expensive subscription to start your family tree. A great deal of Canadian family history is free, from official archives, if you know where to look.

National records

  • Library and Archives Canada is the best free starting point. It has the national censuses, passenger lists and immigration records, land grants in the West, and service files for soldiers of the First World War.
  • The Veterans Affairs Canada website includes the Canadian Virtual War Memorial, with records of Canadians who died in service, often with photos and newspaper clippings added by families.

Provincial archives

Births, marriages and deaths are kept by each province, and older ones are often transferred to the provincial archives, where they become free to search. Start with the province your family lived in:

  • Quebec: Bibliothèque et Archives nationales du Québec
  • Ontario: Archives of Ontario
  • Other provinces: search for “provincial archives” plus the province name, and use the site ending in .gov or .ca run by the province.

Free, non-profit sites

  • FamilySearch is free with an account and has millions of scanned Canadian records, including many church registers.

Local sources people forget

  • Local libraries often have old newspapers with birth notices, wedding announcements and obituaries.
  • Genealogical societies in most regions have volunteers, cemetery transcriptions and parish indexes.
  • Cemeteries and churches keep their own burial and parish books, and many will look something up if you ask kindly.
Before you pay: if a paid site shows you a record, note its source (the archive, parish or year). The original is very often free from the archive that holds it.

Keep everything in one place

Each record you find adds a piece. Save a copy, note where it came from, and attach it to the right person in your tree, so the next person in the family doesn’t have to search again.
